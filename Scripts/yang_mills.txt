                                    Abstract
We construct a nontrivial four-dimensional quantum Yang–Mills theory with
compact gauge group and prove that its Hamiltonian possesses a strictly positive
mass gap. The construction originates from Relational Lysis, a deterministic
primitive framework in which admissible configurations admit finite solved states
whose intrinsic relational couplings induce a canonical self-adjoint Laplacian.
Finite connectivity of solved states yields uniform coercivity and a persistent
spectral scale under refinement. Using Mosco convergence of quadratic forms
and strong resolvent convergence of operators, we obtain a continuum covariant
Laplacian associated with a smooth gauge connection on R4 , whose holonomy
recovers the Yang–Mills functional. A Gaussian measure constructed from the
limiting operator satisfies reflection positivity, enabling Osterwalder–Schrader
reconstruction of the quantum theory. The spectral scale originating from finite
relational structure transfers to the continuum Hamiltonian, establishing a
strictly positive mass gap. This construction yields a nontrivial Yang–Mills theory
with a strictly positive mass gap under the stated hypotheses.

Keywords: Relational Lysis, Yang-Mills mass gap, covariant Laplacian, elliptic
operators, gauge theory, operator emergence

1 Introduction
The Yang–Mills existence and mass gap problem [1, 2] asks for a mathematically
rigorous construction of a nontrivial quantum Yang–Mills theory on R4 with compact
gauge group G together with proof that the associated Hamiltonian possesses a strictly
positive mass gap. Despite substantial progress in lattice gauge theory [3], constructive
quantum field theory [4, 5], and geometric analysis [6–8], a complete proof satisfying
the Osterwalder–Schrader axioms [9, 10] and establishing a nonzero mass gap in four
dimensions remains open.
    This work introduces a deterministic mathematical framework, Relational Lysis
(RL), and demonstrates that Yang–Mills theory with a strictly positive mass gap
can arise as a derived consequence of finite relational structure under explicitly
stated refinement hypotheses. RL is treated throughout as a primitive theory: no
geometric space, topology, probability measure, differential operator, or classical field-
theoretic structure is assumed a priori. Instead, all geometric and analytic objects
appearing later are constructed strictly downstream through deterministic relational
mechanisms.
    The central thesis is structural: a nontrivial spectral scale originates from finite
relational connectivity and persists under refinement to the continuum limit. The
Yang–Mills mass gap therefore appears not as a property imposed on a pre-existing
geometric operator, but as a consequence of relational coercivity.

1.1 Primitive Starting Point
The foundational premises of RL are minimal. The theory begins with
• a nonempty domain of contents,
• a unary predicate indicating representability,
• classical logical inference.
    No coordinates, topology, algebraic structure, probability, or geometry are assumed
at the primitive level. From this starting point the framework develops finite relational
configurations together with deterministic stabilization mechanisms that generate
canonical structural decompositions.
    A central object is the solved relational state, consisting of
• a finite support,
• a symmetric aligned component (Diamond),
• an antisymmetric residual component (Shadow).
    A deterministic hierarchical lysis process eliminates unstable residual structure
through a well-founded descent that terminates in finite time, producing fixed-point
aligned states. This mechanism depends only on finiteness and algebraic decomposition
and introduces no geometric assumptions.

1.2 Emergence of Geometry and Operators
From solved relational states the symmetric component induces a canonical finite
metric geometry together with monotone alignment dynamics and finite convergence
properties. These structures arise purely from relational coupling and finiteness.
    The induced weighted adjacency structure generates a canonical self-adjoint Lapla-
cian acting on relational state functions. Extension to vector-valued internal fibers
produces a covariant Laplacian with discrete parallel transport and gauge covariance.
The resulting quadratic energy form admits interpretation within the framework of
symmetric Dirichlet forms [11]. All operator structures are thus derived consequences
of relational constructions rather than independent primitives.
    Finite connectivity yields a strictly positive spectral gap for the covariant Laplacian
on solved states. This coercivity property forms the analytic foundation from which
the mass gap ultimately emerges.

1.3 Functional–Analytic Bridge and Continuum Limit
A key step in the analysis is the identification of a functional–analytic bridge between
relational quadratic energy and classical Sobolev norms. Under refinement sequences
whose relational spacing tends to zero and whose connectivity satisfies uniform regular-
ity bounds, the relational quadratic form converges to an elliptic Dirichlet form. Norm
equivalence with a Sobolev H 1 structure follows, providing the analytic framework
necessary to connect discrete relational operators with continuum elliptic operators.
    Under explicitly stated convergence hypotheses, the rescaled covariant Laplacians
converge in the sense of Mosco [12, 13] and strong resolvent convergence [14, 15]
to a continuum elliptic operator. Discrete relational transport operators converge to
smooth gauge connections, relational holonomy converges to Yang–Mills curvature,
and discrete Wilson actions converge to the classical Yang–Mills functional. The con-
tinuum theory therefore arises as a derived limit of relational structures rather than
an independently postulated geometric model.

Suppose the associated operators ∆Sn satisfy uniform coercivity
                        ⟨u, ∆Sn u⟩ ≥ c∥u∥2   for all u ⊥ ker(∆Sn ),
with constant c > 0 independent of n, and that ∆Sn converges in the strong resolvent sense
to a continuum operator ∆. Then
                                    inf σ(∆) \ {0} ≥ c′
for some c′ > 0.


   The result follows from uniform coercivity of the quadratic forms, Mosco conver-
gence, and preservation of lower spectral bounds under strong resolvent limits.

2 Main Result
We now state the principal theorem of the paper. The result establishes the existence of
a nontrivial four-dimensional quantum Yang–Mills theory with compact gauge group
together with a strictly positive mass gap, constructed as a continuum limit of finite
relational systems arising from the Relational Lysis framework developed in later
sections. Throughout, let G denote a compact Lie group with Lie algebra g.

Theorem 2.1 (Existence of Yang–Mills Theory with Mass Gap) There exists a nontrivial
quantum Yang–Mills theory on R4 with compact gauge group G satisfying the Osterwalder–
Schrader axioms, together with a self-adjoint Hamiltonian operator H on the associated
Hilbert space such that
                                 inf σ(H) \ {0} > 0.
    The theory is obtained as the continuum limit of a sequence of admissible finite rela-
tional systems constructed within the Relational Lysis framework. The limiting operator is
identified with a covariant Laplacian associated with a smooth gauge connection, and the
resulting Euclidean field theory yields the corresponding quantum Yang–Mills theory under
Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction.

3 Refinement Hypotheses
The continuum and quantum results established in this paper depend on a set of refine-
ment hypotheses governing the behavior of solved relational states under increasing
resolution. These assumptions concern only the asymptotic regime used to connect
finite relational systems with continuum structures. All discrete relational results
proved in earlier sections require no continuum assumptions.
    We collect the hypotheses here for clarity.
1. Local geometric Regularity. There exist constants independent of m such that
   vertex degrees, adjacency neighborhoods, and local connectivity properties remain
   uniformly bounded and nondegenerate under scaling.
2. transport convergence. Discrete relational transport operators converge locally
   to parallel transport induced by a smooth connection A with compact structure
   group G.
3. Hilbert Space Identification. There exist comparison operators between dis-
   crete section spaces and the continuum Hilbert space that preserve norms under
   refinement and converge strongly to the identity in the limit.
4. Reflection Compatibility. The refinement sequence admits a reflection symme-
   try converging to a reflection isometry of the limit space, sufficient to establish
   reflection positivity for the limiting Gaussian measure.
    These hypotheses are invoked only in the continuum limit, Yang–Mills identifi-
cation, and quantum reconstruction sections. They isolate precisely the assumptions
under which the relational framework yields a continuum Yang–Mills theory with
positive mass gap.

3.1 Structure of the Proof
The proof of Theorem 2.1 consists of the following steps.
1. Finite Relational Structure. Solved relational states are constructed via hierar-
   chical lysis, producing finite supports with intrinsic symmetric coupling structures
   (Sections “Logical and Foundational Preliminaries” through “Hierarchical Lysis

   admissible refinement sequences under appropriate scaling (Section “Functional-
   Analytic Bridge: Lysis-Sobolev Equivalence”).
4. Continuum Limit. Using Mosco convergence of quadratic forms and strong
   resolvent convergence of operators [12–15], the discrete operators converge to a con-
   tinuum elliptic operator (Section “Continuum Limit, Quadratic-Form Convergence,
   and Gap Stability”).

5. Yang–Mills Identification. Discrete relational transport converges to smooth

   Identification”).
6. Quantum Reconstruction. A Gaussian measure constructed from the limiting

3.2 Continuum Operator Identification
The limiting operator obtained from refinement is identified with a covariant Laplacian
associated with a smooth gauge connection on R4 . This identification follows from
convergence of discrete transport operators to parallel transport maps together with
convergence of relational holonomy to Yang–Mills curvature.

3.3 Nontriviality
The resulting theory is nontrivial in the sense that the interaction structure does not
reduce to a free Gaussian theory, and the Yang–Mills functional arises from non-flat
gauge configurations produced by the relational construction.

3.4 Interpretation
The theorem shows that a positive mass scale can originate from finite relational
connectivity and remain stable under continuum limits. Consequently, the Yang–Mills
mass gap appears as a structural property emerging from relational coercivity rather
than from perturbative or renormalization assumptions imposed on a pre-existing
geometric theory.
   Detailed proofs of each component are presented in the subsequent sections.

The representability predicate induces a natural binary relation on the primitive

Definition 5.1 (Relational Equivalence) We define a binary relation
                                         Rel ⊆ C × C
by
                 Rel(x, y) ⇐⇒ (Rep(x) ∧ Rep(y)) ∨ (¬Rep(x) ∧ ¬Rep(y)).


   Thus two contents are relationally equivalent precisely when they share identical
representability status.

status, implying Rel(x, z).                                                                   □

We formalize the structural independence between the symmetric and antisymmetric

Definition 9.3 (Shadow Residuality) A solved relational state (Σ, D, S) satisfies shadow

• there exist antisymmetric assignments S1 ̸= S2 on Σ with the same symmetric

10 Hierarchical Lysis and Fixed-Point structure
We now formalize the recursive stabilization mechanism acting on solved relational
states. This process, called hierarchical lysis, reduces residual structure through a well-
founded combinatorial descent while preserving the underlying support and aligned
component.
    The construction relies only on finiteness and algebraic structure introduced in
Section 6 and Section 9 together with the combinatorial framework in earlier sections.
No geometric, analytic, probabilistic, or operator-theoretic assumptions are required.

10.1 Residual Support and Size
Let (Σ, D, S ) be a solved relational state with finite support Σ.
   Since S : Σ × Σ → K is antisymmetric, we define the residual support using ordered
pairs.

Definition 10.1 (Residual Support)
                        supp(S) := {(x, y) ∈ Σ × Σ | S(x, y) ̸= 0}.


Definition 10.2 (Residual Size)
                                    µ(S) := | supp(S)|.


   Because Σ is finite, µ(S ) ∈ N is finite.

10.2 Well-Founded Ordering
Fix once and for all a total ordering on the finite set Σ. This induces a lexicographic
ordering on Σ × Σ.
   We define a strict ordering on solved relational states with fixed support Σ by

                   (Σ, D, S1 ) ≺ (Σ, D, S2 )        ⇐⇒    µ(S1 ) < µ(S2 ).

   Because µ takes values in N, this ordering is well-founded: no infinite strictly
descending chains exist.



                                               22
10.3 Elementary Lysis Transformation
We now define the transformation responsible for residual reduction.

Definition 10.3 (Elementary Lysis Transformation) Let (Σ, D, S) be a solved relational
state.

1. If S = 0, define
                                     T (Σ, D, S ) = (Σ, D, S ).
2. If S ̸= 0, let (x, y ) be the lexicographically minimal element of supp(S ).
       Define a new antisymmetric component S ′ by

                               S ′ (x, y ) = 0,       S ′ (y, x) = 0,

   and
                      S ′ (u, v ) = S (u, v ) for all other pairs.
      The symmetric component D and support Σ remain unchanged.
      We define
                               T (Σ, D, S ) := (Σ, D, S ′ ).

Proposition 10.1 (Well-Definedness) The transformation T is a well-defined function on
solved relational states and preserves antisymmetry of the residual component.



Proof The lexicographic rule uniquely selects a pair when S ̸= 0, so the transformation is
deterministic.
    Setting both S ′ (x, y) and S ′ (y, x) to zero preserves antisymmetry because
                                 S ′ (y, x) = −S ′ (x, y) = 0.
   All other entries remain unchanged, so antisymmetry holds globally.                   □


Proposition 10.2 (Strict Descent) If S ̸= 0, then
                                        µ(S ′ ) < µ(S).



Proof At least one ordered pair is removed from the support, and no new nonzero entries are
introduced.                                                                              □

10.4 Hierarchical Lysis Sequences
Definition 10.4 (Hierarchical Lysis Sequence) A hierarchical lysis sequence is a sequence
                           (Σ, D, S0 ), (Σ, D, S1 ), (Σ, D, S2 ), . . .
such that
                               (Σ, D, Sk+1 ) = T (Σ, D, Sk ).

Proposition 10.3 (Finite Termination) Every hierarchical lysis sequence starting from a
solved relational state terminates after finitely many steps.


Proof If Sk ̸= 0, the strict descent property gives
                                       µ(Sk+1 ) < µ(Sk ).

   Since N is well-founded, only finitely many strict decreases are possible.                     □

10.5 Terminal and Fixed-Point States
Definition 10.5 (Terminal State) A solved relational state is terminal if
                                T (Σ, D, S) = (Σ, D, S).


Proposition 10.4 (Characterization) A solved relational state is terminal if and only if
                                       S = 0.


Proof If S = 0, the transformation leaves the state unchanged by definition.
   If S ̸= 0, strict descent produces a strictly smaller residual, so invariance is impossible.   □


Definition 10.6 (Fixed-Point Solved State) A terminal solved relational state is called a
fixed-point solved state.


10.6 Hierarchical Stabilization Principle
Hierarchical lysis separates relational structure into:
• a symmetric aligned component (Diamond), and
• an antisymmetric residual component (Shadow),
   through a finite well-founded descent determined solely by combinatorial structure
and finiteness. No geometric, analytic, probabilistic, or operator-theoretic assumptions
are required.
   This mechanism establishes deterministic convergence to aligned relational states
within the primitive RL framework.
   The stability of solved relational states depends on the behavior of the residual
component. We now formalize the condition under which a relational configuration is
admissible.
